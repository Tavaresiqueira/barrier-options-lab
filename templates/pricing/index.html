METHODOLOGY & LIMITS

From a vanilla option to a path-dependent structure

The lab follows the same reasoning a structurer uses: establish the vanilla benchmark, add the barrier event, simulate paths, activate the contractual payoff, and finally combine signed legs into a package.

01

Start with the vanilla benchmark

Black–Scholes prices a European call or put from spot, strike, time, DI rate, dividend yield and volatility. Only the terminal price matters. This deterministic value is the control against which the barrier premium is compared.

02

Add a path state

A barrier adds direction, level, knock-in or knock-out behavior, monitoring rules and historical status. Two paths can finish at the same price but produce different payoffs because only one touched the barrier.

03

Build the observation clock

Continuous monitoring watches the full path. Daily, weekly and monthly contracts use B3 sessions; maturity-only observes once. Fewer observation opportunities generally reduce knock-in value and increase knock-out value.

04

Simulate under risk neutrality

GBM grows each path at DI minus dividend yield, with dispersion controlled by volatility. For continuous barriers, a Brownian bridge estimates crossings hidden between simulation points.

05

Apply and discount the payoff

The terminal vanilla intrinsic value is paid only when the barrier state makes the option active. Each path payoff is discounted, averaged and reported with a standard error and confidence interval.

06

Challenge the result

With no rebate, knock-in plus knock-out should recover the vanilla price. The parity residual is compared with Monte Carlo noise. Repeating a seed must reproduce the same result.

Why each parameter matters

Spot

Sets current moneyness and distance to the barrier. Yahoo Finance can update only this field; verify its timestamp before use.

Strike

Controls terminal intrinsic value. For an Up-and-In call with H ≤ K, finishing in the money implies the barrier was crossed under continuous paths.

Barrier

Controls activation or extinction probability. Its effect is path-dependent, not merely a second strike.

Volatility

Widens path dispersion and usually increases crossing probability. Its effect on knock-outs can oppose ordinary vanilla intuition.

DI rate

Changes discounting and risk-neutral drift. It is an explicit manual assumption in this version; Yahoo does not supply the desk DI curve.

Dividend yield

Reduces forward equity growth under the model. It remains an explicit manual assumption and is not inferred from Yahoo.

Monitoring

Defines when a crossing legally counts. Continuous, daily-close and monthly contracts are economically different products.

Barrier status

Represents history explicitly. Current spot cannot prove that a barrier was never touched earlier.

Paths and seed

More paths reduce sampling error; the seed makes a study reproducible and stabilizes solver comparisons.

Collar Up-and-In

The protective put costs premium; the short Up-and-In call finances it only conditionally. Because that call is normally cheaper than a vanilla call, retaining the same put often creates an initial debit.

Fence Up-and-In

The lower short put adds financing but reopens losses below its strike. The two payoff charts show why the short call can cap upside in one path state and disappear in the other.

Nitro — Call Up-and-Out

The client buys a cheaper bullish call because a monitored touch of H permanently eliminates it. Being right about direction is insufficient when the rally is strong enough to cross the barrier.

Double Up KO

This version is S + CUO(K1,H) − qC(K2). If H is touched, the leveraged long call disappears while the underlying and short vanilla call remain.

Zero cost is not zero risk

The solver balances initial option premiums within tolerance. It does not remove downside, barrier, volatility, liquidity, gap, counterparty or model risk.

Model boundary

Constant volatility, constant rate, continuous dividend yield, GBM and European exercise. No jumps, discrete dividends, corporate actions, local/stochastic volatility, credit or funding adjustment, hedging reserve, dealer margin, RFQ or trading.
